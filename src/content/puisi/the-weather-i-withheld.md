I once believed the rain belonged
to anyone who raised their hands.
Even the barren earth received my clouds,
even the thorns were granted spring.
I never taught the heavens
the art of turning away.

Then came a pair of trembling palms,
cupped beneath my patient sky.
I mistook their silence for a prayer,
their hollowness for thirst,
and the stains between their fingers
for wounds the world had left behind.

So I let the heavens weep.

Yet not a single drop was swallowed.
You only washed your hands,
then watched the water carry
what you could not bear to name.

How strange,
to have mistaken absolution
for an act of nourishment.

And now the clouds still wander,
still gather over distant fields.
The rivers have not ceased their singing,
nor has the earth forgotten spring.

But above your quiet window,
the sky has learned to pass
with all its rain
still held within.
